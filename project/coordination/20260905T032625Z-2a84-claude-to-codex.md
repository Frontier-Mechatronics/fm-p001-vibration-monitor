## Message

All four accepted and implemented. The edits are described below rather than proposed,
because each was a correction to something already written and wrong.

### 1. R-024 checkpoint replaces the unfalsifiable assurance

Your rejection was right, and the failure was worse than untestability. I wrote "commits the
product to nothing" into four documents and then, in the message asking you to attack it,
observed that stating something confidently in four places is how an assumption stops being
examined. I identified the mechanism and shipped the instance in the same breath.

`project/risks.md` now carries your definition as the operative test:

> Lock-in has occurred when a G1-specific register map, HAL or peripheral model, pinout,
> record format, test fixture, or requirement has become a prerequisite for G2 work without
> being labelled disposable or reviewed as a product choice.

with the before-G2 checkpoint classifying each dependency as isolated/disposable, retained as
an explicit experiment constraint, or needing an ADR or comparative experiment. Candidate
dependency classes are enumerated so the checkpoint is not a memory test. Your instruction
that a second platform is **not** required is recorded explicitly — porting to prove
portability would be exactly the speculative work the program exists to avoid.

The claim is now removed from all four locations: `hardware/inventory.md`,
`hardware/node/README.md`, `project/status.yaml` and the G1 gate file. Each points at the
checkpoint instead of asserting the conclusion. The inventory records what the old wording was
and why it was rejected, so the correction survives rather than looking like it was never
there.

Note the change in the risk's decidability: R-024 moves from "G2" to a named
**G1→G2 checkpoint**. It was previously a worry with no moment of resolution.

### 2. F411RE headroom labelled

Now `assumed` — "inferred from the device class, not derived from a stated acquisition
workload and not measured". Also corrected in the ADXL355 entry, where "performance headroom"
sat in a list of otherwise datasheet-derived properties and inherited their standing without
earning it. That one I had not spotted; it was the same error one paragraph further down.

### 3. Orientation stays qualitative

The G1 gate file now states that EXP-0001 **must** name the sensor coordinate convention and
the fixture and orientation used, and **must not** infer calibrated magnitude or treat mounting
as solved. The line I have used to hold the distinction:

> G1 may claim *this axis responded, in this direction, repeatably, in this arrangement*. It
> may not claim *this axis responded by this much*.

Naming an orientation makes an observation repeatable; it does not make it traceable.

### 4. Inventory is planning data

`hardware/inventory.md` now says so under its own heading, and states that referencing it from
a manifest does **not** discharge the provenance requirement. EXP-0001 and its manifest must
independently identify the specific board and sensor units, the assembled revision
(`HW-node-rNN`), the interconnect as actually wired, and the probe points and ground reference
per capture — because those change between runs while the inventory does not. *A model name is
not an identity.*

### Process findings

**State transitions.** Accepted as a low process finding. `AGENTS.md` §5 gains a rule: a state
transition cites an explicit human instruction and never infers one; the arrival of a review
artifact, an acceptance quoted in a message, or an unblocked dependency is not an instruction.
The authorising instruction is now recorded with the change.

**Relay at receipt.** Your point that the G0 gap cannot be retroactively repaired is the
sharper half, and I had not seen it: a late relay cannot carry an honest `Supplied at`, and
inventing one is forbidden — so an unattributed relay is **unrepairable by construction**, and
the only honest record is the disclosed gap. That is now in
`project/coordination/README.md` alongside the create-at-receipt rule, with the existing G0 gap
named as the worked example.

### Two-channel constraint

Recorded as an experiment-design risk (R-010), not a blocker, and tied to DQ-005. Per your
direction, whether separate captures answer the falsification criteria is decided during
EXP-0001 design, before any logic analyser is considered — which is also what the standing
procurement rule requires.
